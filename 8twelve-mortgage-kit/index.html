Mortgage
Conversation Guide.

Don't memorize a pitch. Find out what the person is trying to do, then decide whether a personal introduction would help.

Where to start

Renewal“When does your mortgage come up for renewal?”

If soon: “Have you compared your options yet?”

Monthly payments“Are you comfortable with what you're paying each month?”

Ask if their concern is the payment or the full cost.

Buying or moving“Any plans to buy or move in the next year?”

Ask about timing, not a mortgage approval.

Debt / refinancing“What's the main thing you're hoping to change?”

Listen for the goal. Let the specialist weigh the trade-offs.

When they respond

“I'm just looking.”“That's fine. What are you trying to figure out?”

Offer an introduction only if they want help.

“I already have a broker.”“That's good. Is there anything you're still trying to work out?”

Respect their relationship and their answer.

“Who is 8Twelve?”“It's a licensed mortgage brokerage with access to different lenders.”

Explain that your specialist is someone you know. Don't promise the lowest rate.

Asks about joining the teamKeep this separate.

Offer a discussion with a business leader about work, training and qualifications. Don't pivot a client's mortgage need into a pitch.

Personal introduction

“I know a licensed mortgage specialist at 8Twelve. Would you be open to me introducing you so they can review your options?”

Follow through: Ask permission to share a name and chosen contact detail, make the introduction, confirm the connection and agree on a date: “Would Thursday work for me to check back?”

Do not quote rates, promise approval, recommend loans or request confidential underwriting records. 8Twelve is a separate licensed mortgage brokerage. Respect a clear “no.”

Assumptions behind the mortgage, investing and compensation examples. All figures are illustrative.

01 / Mortgage and debt payments

The Toronto family is hypothetical, not a claim about typical households or current market-average rates. Actual balances, rates and payments will differ. The debts move into the mortgage; they aren't erased. Your home secures the combined debt. Fees or penalties may apply. Taking longer to repay your mortgage could mean more total interest. This does not mean you qualify or save overall.

Original debtBalanceAssumed rate and payment basis
Mortgage$750,0004.79% · 25 years
Credit cards$25,50020.99% · payment of 3% of the balance
Car or personal loan$24,0009.99% · 5 years
Line of credit$35,5008.45% · interest-only

Both mortgages assume fixed 4.79% annual interest, compounded semiannually, and 25-year amortization with monthly payments: $4,272.81 on $750,000; $4,757.06 on $835,000. Displayed amounts are rounded to whole dollars. Refinancing costs are paid separately. The $1,041 difference is $12,492 over a year only if it continues for 12 months.

Both payoff comparisons start with the combined $835,000 balance and $4,757.06 monthly payment. Paying $1,041 extra gives about 17.8 years instead of 25 (7.2 years sooner); paying $520.50 extra gives about 4.3 years sooner. This assumes permitted, fee-free prepayments and the same rate through renewals, not a 25-year fixed-rate term. Actual lender terms and renewal rates may change the results; these are not overall consolidation-cost comparisons.

These payments are not a lender quote or an approval. With a federally regulated lender, refinancing is typically capped at 80% of the home's appraised value; $835,000 implies about $1,043,750. A licensed mortgage specialist reviews equity, eligibility, options and full costs.

02 / Regular investing

Hypothetical rates, not guaranteed returns. Assumes $1,041 invested at each month-end from a $0 starting balance for 10 years, with monthly compounding at the 6% annual rate divided by 12: $170,598, shown as $171,000. Values include contributions, are rounded to $1,000, and exclude fees, taxes and inflation. Actual returns vary and can be negative.

The combined choice invests $520.50/month (about $85,299, shown as $85,000) and pays $520.50 extra on the mortgage. These aren't overall gains from consolidation. Continued contributions are assumed; mortgage payments and available cash may change. The original car loan ends after five years, changing the comparison. Returns and payment differences aren't guaranteed; the difference is not assumed to last 10 years.

03 / Roles, advice and compensation

8Twelve is a separate licensed mortgage brokerage. Its licensed specialists provide mortgage advice; the advisor owns the client relationship, introduces you and follows through. An appropriately licensed investment advisor can help you choose investments. Mortgage approval, lower rates and lower overall costs aren't guaranteed. Business-building and recruiting belong to the advisor's own financial brokerage, not 8Twelve. An introduction is not a promise of a commission. This doesn't promise guaranteed clients, mortgage approvals, commissions or success. Results depend on the work you put in.

The compensation examples assume the mortgage partner receives 1% ($5,000) per $500,000 loan. Referral factors are 0.00086 below RVP ($430) and 0.00162 for RVP ($810). One per week means 52 completed $500,000 mortgages: 52 × $430 = $22,360 or 52 × $810 = $42,120, before applicable deductions and expenses. Introductions alone do not earn these amounts. 13% HST is withheld and remitted. This is not guaranteed income or a published current pay schedule. Actual payment depends on funding, current written program terms, eligibility, required disclosures and timing. Verify current terms and public-use authorization; the source schedule is internal-use only.
