# Borrower service and advice demand

Retrieved 2026-10-19. Primary evidence only; project context is not evidence. Two search rounds, eight web calls. Confidence refers to faithful reporting, not causal proof.

## Recommended interpretation

An opportunity exists to guide borrowers through choices and maintain relationships. The evidence does **not** establish widespread dissatisfaction or prove a shortage of capable brokers. Advice demand, uncertainty, and missing follow-up are different measures. Avoid claiming that everyone without follow-up wanted it, or that everyone outside a satisfied category was dissatisfied.

## 1. Latest CMHC evidence: uncertainty persists

- Publisher: Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation. Published May 20, 2026; report specifies fieldwork January 7–February 1, 2026, **n=4,112** Canadian adults, household primary decision-makers, with a purchase/mortgage, renewal, or refinance transaction within 18 months. Subgroup sample sizes are not provided in the retrieved summary.
- **26% of mortgage consumers** experienced concerns or uncertainty during the mortgage process. Among those with concerns, **25%** cited understanding terms and conditions, **24%** timing/deadlines, **23%** trust in the mortgage professional (report p.18).
- **47% of homebuyers** felt concerns/uncertainty during homebuying, down from **62% in 2025** (p.16). This broader homebuying measure differs from mortgage-process uncertainty.
- Contrary evidence: **74%** found the mortgage process easy; **74%** felt they obtained the best mortgage deal for their needs.
- Card: “1 in 4 recent mortgage consumers felt uncertainty during the mortgage process.” Interpretation: patient explanations remain valuable; this is not a service-failure rate.
- Confidence: high. Official landing page and original report corroborate headline results; no independent survey replication. PDF search did not locate broker follow-up or broker-satisfaction measures.
- [Official results and publication date](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-research/surveys/mortgage-consumer-surveys/2026-mortgage-consumer-survey)

## 2. CMHC 2025: direct demand for mortgage professionals

- Publisher: CMHC. Published May 21, 2025; fieldwork January 2025. Almost 4,000 Canadian adults, household primary decision-makers, completed a mortgage transaction within 18 months.
- **70% of mortgage consumers** contacted mortgage professionals to learn about options, compared with **60% in 2024**. For first-time buyers, **81%** contacted a professional. Report p.12.
- **57%** contacted lenders and **51%** brokers; these overlap and must not be added. Contacting a professional does not necessarily mean obtaining a mortgage through them.
- Card: “70% contacted a mortgage professional to understand their options.” Keep the 2025 date visible; a comparable contact measure was not located in the 2026 report.
- Confidence: high, original report; corroboration is internal, not independent replication.
- [Results/methodology/date](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-research/surveys/mortgage-consumer-surveys/2025-mortgage-consumer-survey)
- [Report, p.12](https://assets.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/sites/cmhc/professional/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-research/surveys/mortgage-consumer-surveys/survey-results-2025/mcs-ebook-2025.pdf?rev=d0441f31-639c-43e7-8f80-3b510a0044af)

## 3. Follow-up gap: available primary figure is older (2024)

- Publisher: CMHC; published May 8, 2024, per [official results page](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/housing-research/surveys/mortgage-consumer-surveys/2024-mortgage-consumer-survey). Fieldwork dates not established in this retrieval. Canadian recent mortgage-consumer survey; nearly 4,000 overall per official page; subgroup n not supplied in retrieved passage.
- Among consumers who used a broker, **52% received follow-up contact after purchase**. Among lender users, **27% were contacted after their most recent transaction** (p.17). Phrase the measures as the report does: the broker and lender time/event wording differs.
- A 48% broker non-contact figure is a calculated complement, not separately reported, and may conceal response categories. Prefer the directly reported 52%.
- Contrary evidence: **72%** were somewhat/totally satisfied with their broker; **75%** with their lender. The remaining 28%/25% are not all dissatisfied.
- **59%** preferred meeting face-to-face (p.19). This is older preference evidence, not a 2026 human-versus-AI result.
- Card: “Only about half of broker clients reported follow-up after purchase (CMHC, 2024).” Use only if older evidence is acceptable. Do not relabel as 2025/2026.
- Confidence: high for reported figures; original PDF plus search excerpt agree. No independent corroboration of follow-up found.
- [Original report, pp.17–19](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/blog/2024/-/media/1b29e28201c54c6f8e9b9e4056cc6d4f.ashx)

## 4. FSRA: actual issues coexist with high satisfaction

- Publisher: Financial Services Regulatory Authority of Ontario, with Forum Research. Report cover May 2024; file hosted in April 2025 directory (hosting path is not verified publication date). Fieldwork April 11–May 6, 2024; online, weighted Ontario residents 18+, overall n=4,006; mortgage-brokering consumers **n=858**. Not a national sample; non-random online design.
- **57%** reported at least one issue with a mortgage broker/agent over **five years**; **14%** cited higher-than-expected interest rates (p.106). This broad issue measure includes outcomes that need not be broker misconduct or poor service.
- **71%** satisfied with Ontario mortgage-brokering services, versus **63% in 2022** (p.102). Do not turn 29% into dissatisfied. Chart individual rounded categories need not sum to grouped scores.
- **90%** satisfied with complaint handling among those complaining to broker/brokerage/principal broker, **n=78** (p.109).
- Card, if needed: “Better explanations and expectations can matter.” This is an inference; prefer CMHC uncertainty for a national recruiting slide.
- Confidence: high. Summary and detailed question bases agree. Independent organization corroborates that satisfaction is generally high, but does not replicate CMHC’s specific measures. Screenshot requests attempted; one issue-page request errored; figures checked against extracted original text.
- [Original FSRA report, methodology pp.5–6; results pp.102,106,109](https://www.fsrao.ca/sites/default/files/2025-04/FSRA%202024%20Broad%20Cross%20Sectoral%20Survey_EN.pdf)

## 5. FCAC 2026: comparison and knowledge gaps

- Publisher: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada. Published March 2, 2026; updated June 24, 2026; footer modified June 26. Underlying survey of mortgage holders in Monthly Financial Well-Being Monitor plus interviews; exact sample size and fieldwork dates not stated in retrieved article.
- **20%** of mortgage holders did not compare lenders for their current mortgage; **13%** did not know mortgage rates/terms could be negotiated. **36%** had someone else, such as a broker, compare lenders on their behalf; **48%** compared personally. Multiple selections/weighting mean categories can exceed 100%.
- **13%** named better client service as a future switching motivator, compared with **77%** better interest rates. This is not a present dissatisfaction measure.
- Card: “1 in 5 mortgage holders did not compare lenders.” Interpretation: an opportunity to help compare choices, not proof borrowers are unserved or want a broker.
- Confidence: high for published statistics; moderate for generalizability because article omits detailed sample/fieldwork metadata. Independent federal source supports knowledge-gap narrative; does not replicate CMHC.
- [FCAC: Exploring consumer behaviour in mortgage renewal decisions](https://fcac-research-recherche-acfc.canada.ca/en/canada-finance/data-story-histoire-donnees/?id=c1daed30-f9ef-f011-8407-7ced8de0946a)

## Slide selection

Best current card: CMHC 2026 “1 in 4 felt uncertainty.” Best direct professional-demand card: CMHC 2025 “70% contacted a professional.” Best relationship-service card: CMHC 2024 “52% received broker follow-up,” clearly dated and qualified. Avoid a claim of widespread bad service; recent regulatory evidence points to strong satisfaction alongside specific gaps.
