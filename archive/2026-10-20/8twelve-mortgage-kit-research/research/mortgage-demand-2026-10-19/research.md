# Mortgage demand: three presentation statistics

**Owner-approved update:** The page now uses the stronger $912 billion / 70% / 48% set documented in [stronger-candidates.md](stronger-candidates.md), with numbered references and direct source links in the disclaimer section. The findings below preserve the original research and selection history.

## Recommendation

Use **6.6 million mortgaged households**, **26% reporting concerns or uncertainty**, and **25% choosing a broker for paperwork help**. Together these show a large market and concrete reasons people seek support. They do not establish a shortage of brokers, guaranteed clients or advisor earnings. [1][3][4]

The three cards have different populations. Preserve those differences in the card wording and source notes. No defensible Canadian mortgage-broker-specific paperwork-burden percentage was found in the bounded search.

## Contrary evidence and limits

CMHC also found 74% considered the mortgage process easy. MPC reports 83% of broker users would recommend their broker. Broad claims that brokers provide poor service would misrepresent these results. Support needs and generally positive experiences can coexist. [3][4]

The card figures come from strong primary sources but distinct studies, with no independent replication found for either exact survey percentage. Treat the survey values as source-attributed findings, not universal rates. The market total is not the reachable client pool for one team.

## Market size

Statistics Canada estimated **6,568,430 households** owning their principal residence with a mortgage in Q2 2025, using the estimate published in January 2026. Round to **6.6 million** and keep the observation date visible. These are households, not individual mortgage loans or annual transactions. [1]

Bank of Canada separately described about six million mortgages at federally regulated lenders in September 2024. This corroborates multi-million-loan scale, but does not replicate the household count: lender coverage and unit differ. Do not extrapolate counts using the federally regulated share of mortgage dollar value. [2]

## Demand for clearer answers

CMHC's 2026 survey found **26% of recent mortgage consumers felt concerns or uncertainty during the mortgage process**. The sample was 4,112 adults who were household primary decision-makers and had completed a transaction within 18 months; fieldwork was January 7–February 1, 2026. This is not a broker-dissatisfaction rate. [3]

FCAC separately identified comparison/knowledge gaps: 20% did not compare lenders and 13% did not know rates or terms could be negotiated. This supports the direction of a need-for-clarity narrative, not independent replication of CMHC's 26%. [5]

## Paperwork and workload

MPC/Bond's 2026 survey reports **25% of broker users cited paperwork help as a reason for using a broker**. The finding appears on page 4 of the original report, based on a national survey of nearly 2,000 people conducted February 5–25, 2026. Multiple reasons could be selected. This is consumer demand for paperwork assistance, not the percentage of brokers burdened by administration. No independent study replicating this exact estimate was found. [4]

Canadian broker commentary documents process friction qualitatively, but does not quantify a national workload burden. [6] Broader real-estate surveys and foreign broker surveys were rejected as substitutes; see `digests/workload.md`.

## Page implementation

Page 3 now leads with **“A large market. A need for help.”** Card topics are **A large market**, **Clearer answers**, and **Help with paperwork**. Original source links and scope notes replace the old market-stress citations on page 7. Existing role flow and pay illustration remain separate.

## Open questions

A mortgage-broker-specific Canadian workload percentage would require a representative original broker survey with sample, fieldwork dates and question wording. It remains unsubstantiated. A newer household estimate can replace the explicitly dated Q2 2025 figure when retrieved.
